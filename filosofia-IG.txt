The philosophical entry-point of information geometry is the moment one notices that “to measure how far two probability distributions are apart” is already to take a normative stance on what it means to be “close to the truth.”
Everything else—Riemannian metrics, dual connections, dually-flat manifolds—grows out of that single normative decision.
1. Epistemic humility built into the formalism
Fisher’s information matrix is not dictated by Nature; it is dictated by the requirement that the geometry be invariant under sufficient-statistics reparametrisations ​￼.
Thus the very first step is a Cartesian gesture: before I claim to know the world, I ask which aspects of my description would still look the same if I changed my descriptive coordinates.  The answer is: only those measured by the Fisher–Rao metric.  Geometry is therefore born as the minimal prejudice I am forced to adopt once I admit I am a finite, statistically-scrutinising agent.
2. A built-in duality that refuses ontic monologue
Amari’s α-connections give every statistical manifold a dual pair of geodesics.  The duality is not a mathematical curiosity; it is the formal shadow of the fact that there is no unique “straight line” between partial beliefs.  One geodesic answers the question “how should I update if I keep expected counts fixed?”; the other answers “how should I update if I keep expected entropy fixed?”  To choose either is to reveal a pragmatic interest, not to read a physical fact off the world ​￼.
3. From statistical inference to metaphysical neutrality
Because the same dual structure re-appears in thermodynamics, neural networks, and economic choice, information geometry becomes a lingua franca for systems whose state is uncertainty.  The philosophical shock is that uncertainty is treated as a primary substance, not as a secondary lack of knowledge about an underlying ontic state.  The “entries” to the theory are not particles, fields, or wave-functions, but probability measures—objects whose very identity is entangled with the epistemic situation of the observer.
4. The implicit ethic: minimise the wrongness of one’s own surprise
Every geodesic is a path that minimises an information divergence.  Hence motion on a statistical manifold is literally the ongoing project of becoming less surprised by experience.  The geometry is therefore a technology of self-correction; its basic norm is not “describe the world as it is,” but “arrange your expectations so that future surprises are as small as possible.”  In this sense information geometry is the mathematical crystallisation of Peircean fallibilism.
5. Entry slogan
“Start with the Fisher–Rao metric” is the technical instruction; “start by acknowledging that your only reliable compass is the curvature of your own uncertainty” is the philosophical password.
